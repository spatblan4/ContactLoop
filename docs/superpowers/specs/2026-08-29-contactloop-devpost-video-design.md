# ContactLoop Devpost Demo Video Design

## Goal

Create an approximately four-minute English demo video for the “Agents for Humans” Devpost hackathon. The video should make ContactLoop’s value immediately understandable: helping teachers avoid missing important parent communication follow-ups.

## Narrative approach

Use a **Problem → Demo → Impact** structure. The story begins with the human cost of fragmented communication, demonstrates one complete ContactLoop workflow, and closes by explaining how the product keeps teachers in control while reducing administrative memory load.

## Video flow

1. **Problem (0:00–0:35)**
   Explain that teachers care about following up, but conversations are scattered across calls, notes, messages, and memory. A missed follow-up is a workflow problem, not a care problem.

2. **Product promise (0:35–0:55)**
   Introduce ContactLoop as a tool that turns every important conversation into a visible, trackable loop.

3. **Primary product demo (0:55–3:20)**
   - Open the Dashboard and identify a student who needs follow-up.
   - Open the student detail view and review the relevant context.
   - Click **Call Parent**.
   - Show the real phone receiving the call; do not play conversation audio.
   - Return to ContactLoop and record the communication outcome.
   - Show AI-assisted organization of the conversation into a summary and actionable follow-up.
   - Open Follow-ups and show the resulting task in the teacher’s workflow.

4. **Impact and human control (3:20–3:50)**
   Show that the conversation is no longer dependent on memory. Explain that AI organizes and reminds, while the teacher decides whom to contact, what matters, and what happens next.

5. **Closing (3:50–4:10)**
   Reconnect the product to “Agents for Humans”: ContactLoop does not replace the teacher’s relationship with families; it helps the teacher protect that relationship from being lost in administrative noise.

## Tone and presentation

Use natural spoken English, short sentences, and a calm, human-centered tone. Avoid exaggerated claims about AI. The script should include explicit screen-action cues, pauses for interface loading, and a short transition for the phone-call proof.

## Safety and recording constraints

Use fictional student, guardian, and phone information. Hide personal notifications, real phone numbers, and private contact details in the phone screenshot. Show only a few seconds of the incoming call and no call audio.

## Success criteria

- A viewer understands the problem within the first 35 seconds.
- The main demo shows one continuous workflow rather than disconnected feature tours.
- The real call visibly connects the software workflow to a human action.
- The final Follow-up screen proves that the communication produced an actionable next step.
- The spoken script fits approximately four minutes while leaving buffer inside the five-minute limit.
